Growth dashboard — how it works

This dashboard exists to answer one question first: are we growing the newsletter? Everything else — traffic, campaign performance, mentions — is in service of that.

Maintaining, updating or extending this? The full team guide — how it's built, how the data refreshes, the secrets, the institutional mirror, the weekly report, and how to add or change anything — is in the maintainer's guide (HANDOFF.md) on GitHub.

What it shows and where the data comes from

The Growth dashboard is Vital City's internal, passphrase-protected read on how the organization is growing and who it is actually reaching. It is ordered by priority: growing the newsletter list first, then donors and policy-circle influence, then the content engine that feeds both, with list-health and website-reach signals further down. The "At a glance" tiles up top carry the headline numbers — total subscribers and net growth, new signups, active (engaged) subscribers over 30 days and a year, money raised, social followers, website visitors and average engaged time per piece. Beneath them sit the deeper views: the long view of whole-history trends, a funnel card (visitor-to-subscriber conversion, returning vs new readers and how many notable or government-inbox readers opened a recent email), per-article reading metrics, newsletter performance by year, search performance and media mentions. Throughout, plain-language flags and caveats sit next to the softer numbers so nothing gets over-read.

Everything is pulled live from the tools Vital City already uses: Mailchimp for the list, opens, clicks and engagement; Ghost for members, signup sources and the site's own traffic; Donorbox for online gifts; Google Analytics 4 for multi-year website traffic, engaged-reading time and returning-reader rates; Google Search Console for how people find Vital City on Google; and the public article catalogue plus the contact database to flag which subscribers are notable or work in government. The whole thing rebuilds automatically twice a day on GitHub's servers and is encrypted before publishing, so it can live at a shareable web link yet only opens with the shared passphrase. A few honest limits are worth remembering, and the dashboard says so on the relevant cards: donor totals are online Donorbox gifts only, so checks, wires and grants are not counted; email open rates run high because Apple Mail auto-opens messages, so clicks are the truer signal; and Ghost and Google Analytics count visitors differently, so those numbers show direction over time rather than one exact head count.

Newsletter growth & reach

A weekly chart since January 2025: the blue line is total list size (interpolated weekly), green bars are new signups per week and the orange line is total opens per week (right axis). It shows the list growing and how much of it is actually opening over time. Per-week signup bars only go back to December 2025, when Ghost's per-member event feed begins — earlier weeks are still captured in the blue list-size line, just not broken out as bars.

Nuance: the most recent day or two can understate signups until the next rebuild. Signups come from the merged Ghost + Mailchimp dataset, because Mailchimp's own activity count only sees direct-Mailchimp form signups while most Vital City signups arrive via the Ghost form.

Where signups come from

Also in "Growing the list": a breakdown of which referrer sources and which individual pieces drove recent newsletter signups, from Ghost's signup attribution. It answers what actually converts readers into subscribers — which isn't always what gets the most traffic.

Subscriber quality

Two views of the engaged half of the list:

  • Rating distribution — Mailchimp's 1–5 star score, weighted by open rate and tenure. 4–5★ are people you can count on. A list mostly stuck at 2★ usually means many subscribers are new (no history yet) — which can be healthy.
  • Top email domains — where subscribers' addresses live. Useful for guessing who reads on Apple Mail (auto-opens skew the rate), and for spotting institutional clusters (.edu, .gov).

Active subscribers (MAU and AAU)

The headline KPI row includes two "active subscribers" tiles:

  • Active subscribers · 30d (MAU) — the count of unique people who opened at least one Vital City newsletter in the last 30 days. Not a proxy or a rating estimate: it's the actual union of email addresses from Mailchimp's per-campaign open data across every regular send in the window, deduped. The percentage next to the count is MAU as a share of the current list.
  • Active subscribers · 1y (AAU) — the same calculation across the last 365 days. The gap between MAU and AAU is your "lapsed but reachable" audience — people who used to engage but haven't recently. Re-engagement campaigns target exactly that gap.

Two caveats baked into the tooltips: Apple Mail's privacy auto-opens inflate this number across the industry (since the mail client opens emails on the user's behalf to fetch tracking pixels), so MAU/AAU should be read as a ceiling not a floor. And A/B-test ("variate") campaigns are excluded from the union because Mailchimp tracks openers against the child A/B sub-campaigns rather than the parent; the dashboard shows how many variate sends were skipped if that matters.

The long view

The whole-history picture, near the top of the dashboard. A summary strip of growth multiples (visitors per year, all-time page views, newsletter-list growth), then unique visitors by year (2023 onward, from Google Analytics 4), then the newsletter list by year-end and newsletter performance by year (sends, open rate, total opens, total clicks). Year-over-year change is shown inside each bar; the in-progress year is hatched and left without a comparison.

One number to read carefully: the all-time visitor count is roughly the yearly uniques summed, not a tally of distinct humans — Google Analytics identifies a "user" by a browser cookie that rarely survives across years, so the same person is mostly re-counted each year. The dashboard flags this.

The funnel & who's reading

The on-mission cuts, in a card near the top:

  • Visitor → subscriber conversion, by year — new signups divided by website visitors each year. A cross-source estimate (signups from Ghost/Mailchimp, visitors from GA4, which count differently), so read the direction over the years, not the exact decimal.
  • Returning vs new readers — the share of website visitors who came back, for the last 30 days and by year (GA4). A loyalty signal. It's a floor, not a ceiling: anyone who cleared cookies or switched devices reads as "new" again.
  • Who's reading — the policy circle — how many notable (Wikipedia-flagged) and government-inbox subscribers opened an email in the last 30 days, and their share of the active audience. The two counts are clickable to the named list. This is a floor too: it only counts subscribers matched to a flagged person in the contact database.

Reader attention & most-read pieces

Two article-level views in the "Reach & traffic" section, both from GA4 (which tracked the site before the April 2026 Ghost handoff, so it reaches back further than Ghost's own analytics):

  • Reader attention by piece — for each piece, page views alongside average engaged time per view and total engaged time. Engaged time is the seconds a reader's browser tab was actually focused on the page — a read-depth proxy Ghost can't measure. A views-vs-depth scatter splits pieces into quadrants (big audience and deep read, high-traffic-but-shallow, deeply read by a smaller audience). It's an active-tab measure, not literal eyes-on-page reading — a comparative indicator.
  • Most-read pieces — top pieces ranked by unique visitors, with a toggle for the current year-to-date, prior full years and all time. Section/hub pages are filtered out so it's article-only; a piece that lived at both an old and new URL is merged.

How people find us on Google (Search Console)

In "Reach & traffic": clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average ranking position for the searches that surface Vital City, with a toggle for the last 28 days, 90 days or 12 months and the top queries beneath. Impressions are not total search volume — they count only the times a Vital City page appeared in someone's results, not how often a term is searched overall. Source: Google Search Console, which reuses the Google Analytics service account (no separate credential); data lags about two days.

Editing the media-mentions whitelist

The "Media mentions" sidebar pulls from Google News with site-restricted queries — one query per outlet for the exact phrase "Vital City", one for @vitalcitynyc, and one for any vitalcitynyc.org URL share. This means we only see mentions from outlets we've named in the whitelist; the trade-off is much higher precision (we don't get noise from random sites that use the generic phrase) for slightly narrower coverage.

Adding or removing an outlet is a one-line edit to growth_pull.py, the file that runs in the scheduled refresh:

  1. Open growth_pull.py on GitHub: vitalcity-nyc/vital-city-catalogue → growth_pull.py.
  2. Click the pencil ✏️ icon to edit in the browser, then find the MEDIA_OUTLETS list near the top.
  3. Each line is a tuple: ("domain.com", "Display Name"). To add: copy a line, change the values. To remove: delete the line. To rename for display only: change the second value.
  4. Scroll down and click Commit changes. The next refresh (11:00 or 23:00 UTC) will pick it up. To trigger sooner, go to Actions → network-refresh → Run workflow.

What domain to use: the bare hostname without www. works for most outlets (e.g. nytimes.com). For Substacks, use the publication-specific subdomain (johnkroman.substack.com). When in doubt, try the search yourself first at news.google.com with the query "Vital City" site:DOMAIN — if it returns relevant results, the domain works.

Note on WNYC: WNYC's content frequently mentions Vital City on radio segments, but those audio pieces don't get indexed by Google News (only the show-page transcripts do, when they exist). Most contemporary WNYC + Vital City content surfaces under the gothamist.com domain instead — Gothamist and WNYC share a newsroom (both belong to New York Public Radio), and Gothamist publishes web write-ups for many WNYC stories. So the dashboard relies on the Gothamist search to catch most WNYC-adjacent mentions, and the wnyc.org search catches only the small subset of show pages that explicitly print the phrase. The 2022 launch coverage from "All of It" and "Brian Lehrer" lives there; current radio appearances may be missed.

Mentions an outlet whitelist can’t see

A press whitelist answers one question — did a news outlet we track write about us? An August 2026 audit of Vital City mentions found that the most consequential citations weren’t news mentions at all, and were invisible to the dashboard because they lived on domains it never queried. Three lists in growth_pull.py now cover them, edited exactly the same way as MEDIA_OUTLETS:

  • GOV_DOMAINS — government citations. nyc.gov, the City Council, the Public Advocate, the Comptroller, the state legislature and congress.gov, searched for the exact phrase and for vitalcitynyc.org links. The audit singled out a Public Advocate report citing Vital City research as the strongest influence artifact it found — and the old dashboard could not see it, because .gov domains were not on any list. These appear under Cited in government & policy.
  • REPUBLISHERS — policy institutions. Citizens Budget Commission, Niskanen, Center for an Urban Future, Brennan Center, NYU’s Institute for Policy Integrity and the Substacks that quote us. Being cited in a CBC report is a different currency than a passing news mention; they share the same sidebar section, tagged policy org rather than official.
  • VC_VOICES — our people elsewhere. One Google News query per person, paired with “Vital City”. When an editor is quoted in an outlet we don’t track, the whitelist misses it entirely but the brand still travels. Self-published vitalcitynyc.org results are filtered out. These appear under Our voices elsewhere.

Academic citations come from Google Scholar rather than a domain list — four queries, restricted to 2025 and later. Scholar rate-limits after a handful of queries, so a refresh can come back partial; the sidebar says so when it does. Read the two labels carefully: confirmed means the paper cites a vitalcitynyc.org URL and is a real citation. To verify means only the phrase “Vital City” matched, and “vital city” is ordinary urban-planning English (“a vital city centre”) — those are leads to check by hand, not citations. Never quote the combined total as a citation count.

Podcasts, radio and video are the one channel here that isn't automated, because they can't be. Google News indexes pages, not audio; a radio segment or a YouTube panel usually produces no crawlable page containing the phrase "Vital City", so no whitelist of any length will find it. The audit found a dozen such appearances already on the record and entirely uncounted — Brian Lehrer, FAQ NYC, Inside City Hall, Firewall, plus Mamdani and Patrick Gaspard citing Vital City on the record. Those are now logged by hand in mentions_ledger.json and shown under Podcasts, radio & video, labelled with the date they were last audited so an out-of-date list looks out of date rather than looking empty.

Adding an entry is the same browser edit as the whitelist: open mentions_ledger.json, click the pencil, copy an existing row inside items and fill it in. Required: date (YYYY-MM-DD), outlet, program, url, who, kind (audio or video) and role (appearance when one of ours was on the show, citation when someone else cited us). note is optional and is what the dashboard shows as the one-line summary. Bump last_audit when you do a sweep. Watch the commas — JSON fails on a trailing one, and the section will show as empty if the file won't parse.

Still not fully covered: podcast mentions where nobody from Vital City appears and nobody has told us. A separate audit found appearances Google News does not index at all, because podcast episodes rarely produce a crawlable page containing the phrase. Catching those needs transcript search, which no free feed provides — for now they have to be logged by hand.

Media / social mentions (the sidebar)

Free feeds, narrowed to NYC context so "Vital City" the brand isn't confused with the generic phrase. Coverage quality differs sharply by source — the honest hierarchy:

  • Bluesky (complete) — Bluesky's own search API is open and free (it's an open protocol), so every public post containing "vitalcitynyc" or a vitalcitynyc.org link is captured, with like and repost counts. Posts from @vitalcitynyc.bsky.social are tagged as VC's own; everything else is third-party chatter.
  • Reddit (complete for link posts) — Reddit's free search feed, queried for every submission linking to vitalcitynyc.org plus any post naming the domain. These are usually shares of VC articles by users — useful signal of organic distribution.
  • X, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, Threads (partial) — these platforms sell their search APIs (X Basic alone is ~$100/month), so the dashboard surfaces only what Google's index happens to catch via site-restricted queries. Real but incomplete: assume there is more chatter on these platforms than shown. The "Coverage" note at the bottom of the social list says exactly this.
  • Google News — external publications quoting "Vital City". Self-references are filtered out, items must actually contain the brand name (not just link to the site), and known ephemeral page types (TV schedules like NY1's "News All Day", live blogs) are excluded via an editable junk-title list at the top of growth_pull.py. Link roundups are tagged, not dropped — a daily "Wednesday's Headlines" post linking to us is real distribution, but it isn't a story about us, and counting the two the same way distorts the picture. The 21 Aug 2026 audit found most of Streetsblog's 19 hits were exactly this, which made Streetsblog look like our strongest outlet when Gothamist and Politico actually lead on substance. The feed now shows the split (“N substantive · N link roundups”) and marks each one; the patterns live in ROUNDUP_TITLE_PATTERNS.
  • Google Trends — an embedded interactive widget showing search interest for "Vital City" + NYC disambiguators over the last 12 months. Search interest is relative (0–100 = peak in the window), so use it for shape not absolute volume. Spikes typically trail by 2–7 days after a piece breaks through, so it's useful as a "did people go looking for us?" signal after coverage or a viral post.

X (Twitter) and Instagram numbers now live in their own card in the main column — see the "Social" section.

One optional upgrade worth considering later: Google Programmable Search offers 100 free queries per day against Google's full web index — somewhat better X/LinkedIn coverage than the Google News feed, though still partial; it needs a free API key and a search-engine ID. (The most honest social signal — referral traffic by source, which measures readers who actually arrived rather than scraping mentions — is already shown in the Website traffic card, now that Google Analytics is connected.)

Website visitors — the free 5-minute fix (Ghost staff token)

Ghost already tracks site traffic — unique visitors, page views, sources, top pages — in its built-in Analytics tab (powered by Tinybird). The catch, verified directly against the live site: Ghost only shares those numbers with staff credentials. The integration key this dashboard normally uses gets "no permission" on every stats endpoint. The fix is free:

  1. Open Ghost Admin → Settings → Staff and click your own profile.
  2. Scroll to "Staff access token" and copy it (it looks like id:secret).
  3. Add it as a GitHub secret named GHOST_STAFF_KEY on the repo (Settings → Secrets → Actions) — or hand it to Claude to set up.

On the next scheduled refresh the two "Website visitors" tiles fill in from Ghost's own analytics. Two caveats: the token carries your staff permissions, so treat it like a password (a secret, never in the repo); and Ghost's traffic history only starts at the April 2026 site cutover, so the 1-year tile accumulates over time.

Perspective — how the field is trending

Moved here from the dashboard on 9 September 2026: static industry context, not live data, so it belongs with the documentation rather than among the tiles.

Rough context for reading the numbers above. Benchmarks vary by sector and methodology, so treat them as orientation, not a scorecard.

  • Engagement is beating reach. Across news media, subscriptions and loyal audiences keep growing even as overall traffic shrinks — the resilient model is depth, not scale. (Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025; Digiday, 2025)
  • Email is the retention engine. Readers who regularly open a newsletter are roughly 2× more likely to still be subscribed a year later, and healthy digital-news titles keep monthly churn under ~5%. (INMA, 2025)
  • Nonprofit email norms: ~28–29% average open and ~3.3% click for nonprofits (vs ~21% open for for-profits). Vital City's recent sends run ~31% open (above the nonprofit norm) and ~3% click (about average). (M+R Benchmarks 2025; Nonprofit Tech for Good)
  • Open rates are broken; clicks are the truth. Apple Mail auto-opens (~46% of inboxes) have inflated open rates industry-wide since 2021, so the field now leads with click rate and engaged-reader counts — as this dashboard does.
  • The "willing payer" pool is maturing. Only ~18% of people across wealthy countries (~20% in the US) pay for any online news, and growth is flattening — so for a free nonprofit the lever is an engaged audience and the right readers becoming donors, not a paywall. (Reuters Institute, 2025)
  • For think tanks specifically, influence is about who reads, not how many — reaching decision-makers and the policy community beats raw reach. (Vital City tracks this directly in the "who's reading" card above.)
Static context, not live data — last reviewed June 2026. Refresh when the new annual reports land: Reuters Institute Digital News Report · M+R Benchmarks.

How often it refreshes

Twice a day, at 11:00 and 23:00 UTC (≈ 7am and 7pm ET). The same GitHub Action that updates the Contact tool also runs growth_pull.py → encrypt_growth.py and pushes the new encrypted file. The second run is mainly for resilience — GitHub's scheduler often delays or skips cron jobs, so the evening run catches a missed morning one. The "Last refresh" timestamp at the top of the dashboard tells you exactly when this snapshot was taken.

Confidentiality

This page uses the same passphrase as the Contact tool and the same end-to-end encryption (AES-256-GCM, PBKDF2-SHA256 600k rounds). The dashboard contains aggregate metrics but no PII; it's password-protected anyway to keep our growth numbers internal.
